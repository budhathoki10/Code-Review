Large PR Handling: Graphite vs. CodeRabbit
Scenario: a PR touching 60 files, ~1,500 lines changed in each.
Compiled 2026-08-24.

===========================================================
CODERABBIT — engineers around the problem
===========================================================
Treats this as a technical scaling problem to solve, and has published real
detail on how:

- COMPRESSION BEFORE THE MAIN MODEL: a cheaper model shrinks large files
  down to the functions/sections actually touched, before the expensive
  review model ever sees them.
- SCOPE REDUCTION: path filters drop generated/vendored files from
  consideration; "differential analysis" means a re-review only looks at
  what changed since the LAST review, not the whole PR again.
- SANDBOXED INVESTIGATION: an isolated microVM per review, with an agent
  that runs its own grep/ast-grep-style commands to gather only the
  context it decides it needs — not the whole repo.
- RATE-LIMITED AT SCALE: their own engineering blog ("Squeezing Water from
  Stone") admits 50+ file PRs at peak hours hit real AI-provider rate
  limits — they built "Aperture," a token-bucket load balancer that
  prioritizes requests (chat over review, paid over free), rather than
  just buying more capacity.
- OWN ADMITTED LIMIT: CodeRabbit's docs still recommend breaking a huge
  review into smaller chunks / targeted comment threads — even their
  engineering doesn't claim to fully solve arbitrarily large PRs, just to
  degrade more gracefully than a naive tool.

Bottom line: CodeRabbit assumes big PRs will keep happening and invests in
infrastructure (compression, sandboxing, rate-limit orchestration) to cope.

===========================================================
GRAPHITE — designs the problem away
===========================================================
Does not publish a compression/chunking algorithm. Its answer is
architectural, not engineering:

- STACKED PRs IS THE ANSWER: Graphite's core product is breaking a branch
  into a series of small, dependent PRs (each built on the last) instead
  of one giant one. A 60-file change is meant to become ~however many
  small stacked PRs, each reviewed independently while you keep working
  on the next.
- OWN STATED REASONING: Graphite's own materials say a 1,000+ line diff
  "overwhelms the context window, causing the model to lose coherence,
  miss connections between changes, and fall back on pattern matching for
  style issues." That's not a bug they're fixing — it's their justification
  for why you shouldn't have that diff at all.
- REVIEWER (DIAMOND / GRAPHITE AGENT): codebase-aware, not diff-only, via
  a "hybrid strategy" that avoids full-repo indexing overhead — positioned
  as a middle ground between diff-only and full-repo-indexed review. No
  published token-budget/chunking mechanism for an oversized diff.
- NAMING NOTE: "Diamond" was folded into the unified "Graphite Agent"
  brand as of October 2025 — same capability, different name in newer
  material.

Bottom line: Graphite's bet is prevention over compression. If you open a
60-file/1,500-line-each PR without stacking it, there's no disclosed
graceful-degradation path the way CodeRabbit has one — their own stated
position is that the review would simply degrade.

===========================================================
THE ACTUAL DIFFERENCE
===========================================================
Same problem, two different philosophies:

  CodeRabbit: "Big PRs will happen. Build infrastructure to review them
  as well as possible anyway."

  Graphite:   "Big PRs shouldn't happen. Build a workflow (stacking) that
  prevents them, and let review quality assume PRs stay small."

Neither is more "correct" — CodeRabbit's approach works regardless of team
habits but costs real engineering (and real AI spend) to run; Graphite's
approach is cheaper to build but only pays off if a team actually adopts
stacked PRs as a habit, which is a workflow change, not just a tool swap.
